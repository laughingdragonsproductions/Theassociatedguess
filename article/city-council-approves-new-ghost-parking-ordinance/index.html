City Council Approves New Ghost Parking Ordinance

Designated apparition stalls will reduce driveway haunts and meter disputes, officials say, though enforcement remains largely faith-based.

By Tomás Reyes · Friday, January 2, 2026 · MERIDIAN · 1 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

MERIDIAN - The City Council voted 7–1 Tuesday to establish the nation's first municipal ghost parking program, creating twelve designated stalls where spirits may leave their apparitions without blocking driveways, bike lanes, or community theater fundraisers.

Transportation Director Gail Pomeroy said complaints about "unauthorized ectoplasmic storage" rose 40% last year, particularly in historic districts where ghosts prefer Victorian architecture and parallel parking.

Under Ordinance 118-C, each stall is marked with reflective paint visible only on foggy nights and equipped with a timer that resets when the entity achieves closure. Violators receive warnings delivered through cold spots in their former bedrooms.

Council member Harold Finch cast the lone no vote, arguing the city should invest in living residents first. "My constituents cannot find parking after 6 p.m.," Finch said. "Now we are reserving spaces for people who do not pay property tax because they predate the assessor's office."

Pomeroy countered that ghost parking fees - collected in vintage coins and unresolved emotional debt - project to fund two crosswalk signals and a public fountain that weeps on anniversaries.

Meridian's Ghost Relations Office will issue translucent permits after a brief interview confirming the applicant is deceased and not merely "very tired." Commercial hauntings require additional liability insurance.

Local business owners expressed cautious optimism. Café owner Lena Ortiz said phantom double-parking had cost her a delivery ramp. "If they have a spot, maybe they stop hovering near the espresso machine," she said.

The ordinance takes effect next month. Until then, spirits may continue using existing rules: first come, first scream.
