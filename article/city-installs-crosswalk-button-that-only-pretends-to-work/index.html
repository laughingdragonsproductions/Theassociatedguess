City Installs Crosswalk Button That Only Pretends to Work

Transportation officials say illusion of control reduces jaywalking and 'general civic screaming.

By Claire Undertow · Saturday, June 27, 2026 · 1 min read

PORTLAND — The Bureau of Transportation replaced twelve downtown crosswalk buttons with placebo units that click satisfyingly but do not affect signal timing, a pilot program officials said improved pedestrian compliance by 18%.

"People need to feel heard," said engineer Lila Gant. "They don't need to actually be heard by the traffic algorithm."

Signs explain the buttons provide "emotional right-of-way" while lights continue on their scheduled compassionless cycle. Focus groups rated the experience "fine, I guess" and "better than yelling at SUVs."

Behavioral scientists note placebo infrastructure elsewhere: office thermostats, elevator close doors, and congressional ethics committees. Gant said crosswalk buttons were the lowest-hanging fruit of benign deception.

Advocates for disability access raised concerns until learning audible signals remain functional and tied to real controllers. Only the plastic finger toy part is fake.

Tourism board marketed the buttons as interactive art. Instagram posts tagged #IWaitedAnyway increased foot traffic to cafes near intersections, which merchants called a win.

If successful, the city will expand placebo infrastructure to include a community suggestion box that shreds input and plays a soft chime.

Critics demanded real control. Gant offered them a button.
