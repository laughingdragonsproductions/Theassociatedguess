Digital Furniture Takes Over Living Rooms

Holographic sectionals arrive with zero delivery fees and one hundred percent chance of sitting on the cat.

By Riley Dunn · Thursday, April 23, 2026 · SAN FRANCISCO · 1 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

SAN FRANCISCO - Interior design startup RenderHome reported 400% growth after launching digital furniture subscriptions that let renters furnish apartments using only projectors, apps, and denial.

For $29 monthly, customers receive a holographic sectional, a coffee table that updates seasonally, and a bookshelf that displays books they intend to read. CEO Mia Park said the model solves "moving day trauma and also owning a truck."

Traditional retailer Sarah Thompson remained skeptical. "Why would anyone want a virtual coffee table when you can have wood?" she asked. Park replied that wood cannot be swapped to coastal grandmother at midnight.

Early adopters praised customization until a software update replaced everyone's dining chairs with stools labeled beta. Support tickets spiked; RenderHome fixed the issue by rebranding stools as "minimalist lifestyle seating."

Physical therapists reported new injuries from people attempting to lean on nonexistent armrests. Orthopedists call the phenomenon "phantom lounge."

Landlords expressed mixed feelings. Digital furniture leaves no scuffs but also no proof tenants didn't sublet to twelve holographic roommates.

Environmental analysts noted reduced shipping emissions and increased electricity use from GPUs running credenzas at 4K.

Park announced a partnership with a mattress company for hybrid sleep: real pillow, virtual duvet, emotional support throw that exists only on Tuesdays.

Thompson launched a competing campaign: Sit On Something Real. Sales of beanbags rose 6%, which Thompson called victory.

RenderHome closed its seed round and immediately projected a living room on the conference table. Investors called it immersive. The cat disagreed.
