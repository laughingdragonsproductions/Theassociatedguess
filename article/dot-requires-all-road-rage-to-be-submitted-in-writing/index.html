DOT Requires All Road Rage to Be Submitted in Writing

New rule aims to reduce highway incidents by adding a three-business-day comment period.

By James Holloway · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · 1 min read

ATLANTA - The Department of Transportation announced that effective next month, all motorist anger must be filed through Form 1080-RAGE, including timestamp, lane number, and a brief statement of grievance no longer than two expletives.

Secretary Renata Cole said the policy modernizes conflict resolution. "Honking is performance art," Cole said. "We need documentation."

Drivers must pull into designated Expression Lanes, scan a QR code, and await email confirmation before gesturing. Appeals may be submitted within five days if the other party also submits, triggering a mediated Zoom with mute enforced.

Pilot states report mixed outcomes. Utah saw a 30% drop in visible fury and a 300% rise in passive-aggressive bullet points. New Jersey declined to participate, citing "constitutional issues and also habit."

Automakers are updating dashboards with rage autofill. Ford's 2027 model will suggest draft language based on following distance.

Critics say bureaucracy will not stop tailgating. Cole agreed, but noted "at least tailgaters will know we have their email."

Printed forms available at rest stops, between brochures for scenic overlooks and regret.
