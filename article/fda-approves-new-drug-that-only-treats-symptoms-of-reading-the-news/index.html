FDA Approves New Drug That Only Treats Symptoms of Reading the News

Clinical trials show a 68% reduction in the urge to refresh, with mild side effects including believing everything will probably be fine.

By Dr. Helen Marsh · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · 1 min read

SILVER SPRINGS - The Food and Drug Administration approved CalmScroll™ on Monday, the first prescription medication indicated specifically for acute and chronic symptoms associated with reading the news, including elevated pulse, doom-scrolling, and the sensation that it is only Tuesday.

"Patients reported a marked decrease in opening twelve tabs 'just to understand one thing,'" said FDA spokesperson Renata Cole. "We are not treating the underlying events. We are treating the experience of learning about them before coffee."

The once-daily pill works by mildly suppressing the amygdala's response to push notifications and by introducing a temporary belief that someone competent is handling things, even when evidence suggests otherwise.

Side effects include optimism, forgetting why you were angry, and in 4% of trial participants, accidentally liking a relative's post about politics. CalmScroll is contraindicated for journalists, pollsters, and anyone whose job requires knowing what happened.

Pharmaceutical giant Wellstead priced the drug at $890 per month, or "roughly what Americans already pay in emotional rent," according to a press release that analysts called "on brand."

Public health advocates criticized the approval. "This is a bandage on a fire hose," said Dr. Amir Qasim of the Civic Attention Institute. "We should regulate the news, not sedate the audience."

Wellstead countered that CalmScroll pairs well with its companion app, which replaces headlines with photos of otters holding small rocks. Early adopters report sleeping through entire election cycles, which the company lists under Success Stories.

Insurance coverage remains limited. Most plans will pay if a physician documents "significant Twitter exposure," defined as more than six quote-tweets before noon.
