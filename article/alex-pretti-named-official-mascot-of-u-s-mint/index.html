Commerce Department Classifies Switch 2 Zelda Edition as 'Situation We Will Monitor'

Analysts said Zelda Switch 2 restock retailers: Where to buy the 40th anniversary console fits a category of developments that require at least three press releases and no decisions.

By Rhett Caliper · Thursday, November 5, 2026 · LANSING · 2 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

LANSING - In an unprecedented move that has left many scratching their heads, the U.S. Treasury Department has officially named Alex Pretti as the new mascot for its coin series. The announcement was made on Wednesday at a press conference held in the grand hall of the Treasury Building, where officials unveiled a shiny new quarter featuring a stylized image of Mr. Pretti.

"Alex Pretti is not only a beloved figure in our community," said Treasury Secretary Jane Smithson, "but his unique presence will help bring a smile to people’s faces while they handle their hard-earned cash."

Local resident Sarah Johnson, who has been following the controversy surrounding Alex's status as an unofficial mascot, expressed her mixed feelings. "It’s kind of bizarre," she said. "I mean, I get that he makes people happy, but is this really the best use of taxpayer dollars?"

The decision to name Pretti as official mascot was met with some skepticism from fiscal watchdogs. "While we understand the Treasury Department's desire to boost public engagement and morale," noted Emily Greenfield, a spokesperson for the Committee on Financial Oversight, "it’s unclear how much bang we’re getting for our buck."

Alex himself, who has been seen sporting a new set of coins in his pocket, seemed unfazed by the announcement. "I never thought I’d be gracing quarters and half dollars," he said with a grin. "But hey, if it means people are smiling, then I’m all for it."

The fallout from this decision is already being felt across the country. In Michigan, local businesses have started to incorporate Alex-themed merchandise into their product lines, while in other states, there has been a surge in demand for coin collectors’ sets featuring his likeness.

However, not everyone is on board with the new mascot. "I don’t see what’s so special about this guy," said John Williams, a resident of Washington D.C., who was quoted as saying he preferred traditional patriotic symbols like eagles and liberty figures. "He looks more like a caricature than anything else."

As the Treasury Department prepares to roll out the new coin series, officials are urging the public to embrace the change with open hearts. "Remember," said Assistant Secretary Thomas Johnson, "every time you hold one of these coins, you’re holding a piece of Alex’s legacy." The first batch of coins is expected to hit circulation next month, and some collectors have already started hoarding them in anticipation.

For now, Alex Pretti remains the unlikely face of American coinage, a testament to the enduring power of community spirit and perhaps a reminder that sometimes, a little whimsy can go a long way.
