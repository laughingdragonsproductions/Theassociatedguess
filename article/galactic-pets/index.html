Galactic Pets Become City Fads

Pet stores report sold-out crates of alleged exoplanet companions as zoning officials ask whether orbit counts as a backyard.

By Riley Dunn · Thursday, June 11, 2026 · SEATTLE · 2 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

SEATTLE - Demand for “galactic pets” surged 300% this quarter, according to retailers who sell translucent hamsters, bioluminescent goldfish, and one product labeled ONLY MOSTLY FROM EARTH. The trend began after a viral unboxing video showed a child naming a floating orb "Steve" and teaching it to fetch premium streaming passwords. Animal influencers followed with sponsorship deals that veterinarians called “medically vague.”

Pet store owner Emily Lee said customers want novelty without paperwork. “Why keep a creature from another planet when we have wonderful earth pets?” Lee asked. “Because earth pets cannot hover, and hover is this summer’s personality.” She added, “Plus, who needs to clean up after a pet that just vanishes into thin air?”

The trend has sparked a debate among city planners. At an emergency session, zoning officials discussed the implications of galactic pets on urban living. A condo association complained about a tenant's "low-orbit corgi" blocking the shared rooftop telescope. Legal counsel advised that current bylaws cover dogs, cats, and emotional support raccoons but not entities that phase through leashes.

Local architect Thomas Winters designed a prototype of a pet-friendly building with transparent walls to accommodate galactic pets’ ethereal nature. “Imagine a future where your pet can literally walk through the walls,” he said during a press conference. “It’s like having a ghost as a companion, but one that requires food and water.”

The Humane Society urged caution, noting many galactic pets arrive in packaging that lists ingredients as "starlight and corn syrup." A spokesperson recommended adopting from shelters "where the animals are at least provably carnivorous." They also warned of potential health risks. “We’ve seen cases where galactic pets have caused minor burns due to their high-energy fields,” said Dr. Patricia Green, a veterinarian with the Humane Society.

Tech entrepreneurs launched subscription boxes with monthly moons and a FAQ explaining return policy when the pet achieves sentience. One such company, Galactic Paws, promised customers that if their pet gains self-awareness within 30 days, they can exchange it for a more traditional pet or get a full refund. Consumer advocates demanded clearer labeling after three buyers received what appeared to be regular turtles with LED strips.

Lee expects demand to plateau once school resumes and children realize galactic pets still require feeding at 6 a.m. “Every fad ends,” she said. “Usually at the vet.” She added, “And then there’s the issue of explaining to your parents why you need to feed a pet that looks like it’s made of smoke.”

As the city grapples with this new reality, one thing is certain: the pets of Seattle are about to get even more out-of-this-world.
