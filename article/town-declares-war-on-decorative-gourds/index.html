Town Declares War on Decorative Gourds

Council cites 'autumn creep' after pumpkin appears in store aisle on August 3.

By Claire Undertow · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · 1 min read

HARVEST GLEN - The town council voted 5–2 Tuesday to ban premature display of decorative gourds before September 15, a measure sponsors called "a stand against seasonal gaslighting."

"We are still mowing lawns," said Councilmember Bret Ullman. "Why is there a corn stalk next to sunscreen?"

Violators - primarily big-box retailers - face fines of $500 per squash visible from the parking lot. Exemptions exist for pie, which council agreed "has earned its place year-round."

Local farmers supported the ban while quietly shipping early pumpkins to neighboring towns without ordinances. "We respect art and commerce," said grower Hal Voss. "Also rent."

Psychologists testified that autumn creep contributes to temporal whiplash. "People buy hoodies in ninety-degree weather and then feel betrayed by their own closets," said Dr. Priya Nand.

Retail lobbyists argued consumers want fall early. A survey commissioned by the Gourd Association found 62% of respondents "enjoy vibes" and 38% "would like to finish one season before starting another."

Enforcement begins with warnings, then confiscation. Confiscated gourds will be held in a municipal barn and released ceremonially on the equinox, unless they soften first.
