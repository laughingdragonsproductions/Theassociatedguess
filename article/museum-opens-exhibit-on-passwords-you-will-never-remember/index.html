Museum Opens Exhibit on Passwords You Will Never Remember

Curators say collection spans 'childhood pet names' through 'corporate SSO hellscape.

By Claire Undertow · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · 1 min read

PHILADELPHIA - The Franklin Annex opened Forgotten Keys Tuesday, an interactive exhibit chronicling humanity's doomed relationship with authentication from 1994 through whatever yesterday's reset email was about.

Visitors enter through a replica AOL login screen and proceed through eras: the Post-It Period, the Capital-Letter-Number-Symbol Mandate, and the Current Age of Face ID That Fails When You Look Tired.

Curator Hal Voss said the show is part history, part intervention. "We display the password people reused for banking and pizza," Voss said. "Then we gently escort them to a help desk."

Interactive stations let guests test how long it takes hackers to guess "Summer2020!" and how long it takes humans to admit they still use it. A children's wing explains two-factor authentication using puppets named Token and Regret.

Cybersecurity sponsors provided funding but requested their logo not appear near the chapter on plain-text storage. Museum ethics board approved anyway, citing "educational value and mild public shaming."

Gift shop items include a notebook that burns itself after three entries and a mug reading I Heart KeePass, Which I Will Install Tomorrow.

Attendance exceeded projections. Voss attributed interest to "collective trauma and free Wi-Fi."

The exhibit runs through January or until everyone switches to passkeys, whichever fantasy occurs first.
