Braves' Magic Number Officially Set to Appear in Dreams Tonight

Baseball analysts predict the mystical number will visit players’ subconscious as they try to secure their playoff spot.

By Owen Pike · Friday, September 18, 2026 · LANSING · 2 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

LANSING - In an unprecedented turn of events on Wall Street, the Dow Jones Industrial Average surged over 1,000 points after Congress passed a controversial bill dubbed the "Zombie Bond Act." The legislation allows for the issuance of bonds backed by undead investors, effectively flooding financial markets with cash that can only be spent on... well, you know.

Local resident and zombie enthusiast, Victor Wraith, expressed his delight at the new development. "Finally," he said, "we have a legitimate way to invest in our future. I mean, what else are we supposed to do? It's not like we can go out for a walk or anything."

Congressman Ezekiel Vampyr, who sponsored the bill, defended the legislation during a press conference. "Our constituents are tired of being ignored," he stated. "The Zombie Bond Act addresses their unique financial needs and provides stability to our economy. We're just giving them an outlet for their resources in a way that benefits everyone."

According to market analysts, the zombie bond market is expected to grow exponentially as more undead investors seek ways to manage their estates while still contributing to the economy. "It's a win-win," said financial advisor Dr. Edward Mortis. "The living can invest, and the zombies get something to do besides wandering around. It’s like having a second-hand car for your ghost."

Despite the positive sentiment, some remain skeptical about the long-term effects of this new financial instrument. "I'm not sure if I want to see my retirement funds tied up in something that might never die," said Jane Doe, a resident of Detroit. "But then again, I've been saying that about everything since 2014."

As the zombie bond market continues to grow, experts predict it could have far-reaching implications for both traditional investment strategies and the overall health of the economy. One thing is certain: Wall Street has never been more... undead.
