Viral Video Contest Heats Up

City offers $10,000 for the best 24-hour clip; ethics board offers aspirin.

By Riley Dunn · Thursday, April 9, 2026 · LOS ANGELES · 3 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

LOS ANGELES - The Office of Civic Engagement launched its first Viral Video Challenge, promising $10,000 to whichever resident produces the most-shared clip within 24 hours of posting, a metric officials called "democracy but louder." Contestant Sarah Johnson uploaded a story about adopting a shelter dog who immediately learned to open credit apps. "Authenticity wins," Johnson said, while staging take forty-one.

The contest attracted a diverse array of entries, from the mundane to the bizarre. One contestant proposed a solution for potholes by suggesting driving over them faster. Another video showed a heated argument between two cars parked in front of a fire hydrant, with subtitles that read "Parking Disputes Set to Trending Audio." A third entry featured a man explaining taxes through interpretive dance, which was met with mixed reactions from the judges.

Judges include a mayor's aide, a local comedian, and an algorithm intern who has never been outdoors. Criteria list originality, community spirit, and "does not require context from a therapist." The mayor’s aide seemed particularly enthusiastic, often quoting obscure political slogans while critiquing entries. The local comedian offered witty commentary but struggled to maintain a straight face during the more absurd submissions. The algorithm intern, however, remained stoic throughout, occasionally nodding in agreement with his own logic.

Critics called the contest a distraction from potholes. Spokesperson Dana Ruiz said potholes are also content if filmed vertically. "We're not ignoring infrastructure," she explained, "but sometimes it's fun to look at things differently." The city council responded by allocating funds for next year’s prize from the savings of not fixing the fountain that would have been a great backdrop.

Entries flooded in: proposal fails, parking disputes set to trending audio, and a man explaining taxes through interpretive dance. The fire department asked participants to stop using emergency sirens as beat drops. "We need our sirens for emergencies," said Captain Mike Thompson, "not for your viral video." Participants were warned that the use of sirens could result in fines or even a ban from future contests.

Brand sponsors offered parallel prizes for product placement disguised as personality. One coffee shop won accidentally when a cat knocked over a latte in slow motion. The owner, Sarah Lee, was thrilled but baffled by the win. "I just wanted to make sure my cat got enough water," she said. Another sponsor, a local tech company, offered a prize for the best use of their new social media analytics tool. One participant used it to create a video showing a smartphone app that could predict earthquakes based on dog barks.

Mental health advocates recommended sleep. Participants recommended Red Bull. "We need energy," said one contestant who stayed up all night editing his entry, "or else we'll just be posting cat naps instead of viral clips." The city council responded by announcing a midnight deadline, then extended it after realizing time zones are a social construct and also because the mayor wanted to film something with a drone.

The contest entered its final stretch as entries poured in from across the city. A group of high school students filmed their teacher attempting to teach algebra through interpretive dance, which garnered surprisingly many views. Another entry showed a man trying to convince his cat that it was raining outside by playing rain sounds on his phone. The cat seemed unimpressed.

Ruiz announced a midnight deadline, then extended it after realizing time zones are a social construct and also because the mayor wanted to film something with a drone. "We're just making sure everyone has an equal chance," she said, though some participants speculated that this was more about giving the mayor enough footage for his next campaign video.

Winner to be announced Friday, pending verification that views are human and not a bot farm named after a Greek letter. The city council already budgeted next year's prize from the savings of not fixing the fountain that would have been a great backdrop. "We'll just keep moving funds around," said Councilwoman Jane Smith, "until we find the perfect balance between infrastructure and viral content."
