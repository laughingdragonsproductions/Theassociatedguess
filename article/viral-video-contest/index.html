Viral Video Contest Heats Up

City offers $10,000 for the best 24-hour clip; ethics board offers aspirin.

By Riley Dunn · Thursday, April 9, 2026 · LOS ANGELES · 1 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

LOS ANGELES - The Office of Civic Engagement launched its first Viral Video Challenge, promising $10,000 to whichever resident produces the most-shared clip within 24 hours of posting, a metric officials called "democracy but louder."

Contestant Sarah Johnson uploaded a story about adopting a shelter dog who immediately learned to open credit apps. "Authenticity wins," Johnson said, while staging take forty-one.

Judges include a mayor's aide, a local comedian, and an algorithm intern who has never been outdoors. Criteria list originality, community spirit, and "does not require context from a therapist."

Critics called the contest a distraction from potholes. Spokesperson Dana Ruiz said potholes are also content if filmed vertically.

Entries flooded in: proposal fails, parking disputes set to trending audio, and a man explaining taxes through interpretive dance. The fire department asked participants to stop using emergency sirens as beat drops.

Brand sponsors offered parallel prizes for product placement disguised as personality. One coffee shop won accidentally when a cat knocked over a latte in slow motion.

Mental health advocates recommended sleep. Participants recommended Red Bull.

Ruiz announced a midnight deadline, then extended it after realizing time zones are a social construct and also because the mayor wanted to film something with a drone.

Winner to be announced Friday, pending verification that views are human and not a bot farm named after a Greek letter.

City council already budgeted next year's prize from the savings of not fixing the fountain that would have been a great backdrop.
