Company Mandates Camera-On for 'Authentic Disconnection

Leadership says seeing colleagues nod during layoffs 'preserves culture.

By Marcus Tweed · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · 1 min read

SEATTLE - CloudPath Inc. will require cameras on during all meetings longer than eight minutes starting Monday, a policy HR described as fostering authentic disconnection in an era of remote work.

"Audio-only lets people secretly reorganize their pantry," said Chief Culture Officer Dana Hooper. "We need to see the human face that agrees things are fine."

Employees must maintain visible eye contact with the lens at least 60% of the time, verified by software that also detects pets, children, and existential drizzle outside windows.

Violators receive gentle reminders, then manager coaching, then assignment to a focus group about focus. Union organizers called the rule surveillance cosplaying as teamwork.

CEO Gil Marr defended the move in an all-hands where 400 tiles displayed various degrees of compliance. "We are one family," Marr said, while chat filled with messages the presenter could not see.

Productivity metrics rose 3% in pilot groups, mostly from people too afraid to leave their chairs. Well-being surveys dipped, which Hooper attributed to "adjustment lag."

Optional background templates include tasteful bookshelves and a new scene called Honest Kitchen, featuring unwashed mugs and a visible to-do list that will never shrink.

CloudPath says camera-off exceptions require medical documentation or proof of bad hair day reviewed by committee.
