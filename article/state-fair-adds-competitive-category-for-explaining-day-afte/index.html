State Fair Adds Competitive Category for Explaining Day After Tomorrow to Relatives

Judges will score contestants on clarity, patience, and whether anyone still cares about Jake Gyllenhaal's 123-Minute Sci-Fi Action Thriller Is Officially the Biggest Mo by dessert.

By Claire Undertow · Sunday, November 8, 2026 · LANSING · 2 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

LANSING - This year’s State Fair in Michigan has added a new and highly anticipated category to its roster of traditional agricultural and culinary competitions: explaining *The Day After Tomorrow* to relatives who haven’t seen it since 1998. Judges will score contestants on clarity, patience, and whether anyone still cares about Jake Gyllenhaal's 123-minute sci-fi action thriller.

"Imagine a scenario where your cousin in Ohio calls you from the middle of nowhere and demands an explanation for why *The Day After Tomorrow* was such a groundbreaking film," said Judge Sarah Peterson. "We want to see contestants who can hold their own against the relentless barrage of questions, from 'What's with all those icebergs? ' to 'Is that supposed to be realistic?'"

Contestant Mark Thompson, a retired high school science teacher, is taking the competition seriously. "I’ve been preparing for this," he said, referring to his extensive notes and rehearsed responses. "It’s not just about weather phenomena; it’s about climate change denial and the perils of ignoring scientific warnings."

But the real challenge lies in keeping the audience engaged without making them feel old or out-of-touch. Contestant Lisa Johnson, a self-proclaimed *Day After Tomorrow* aficionado, said, "I’ve been watching this movie every year since it came out. It’s like a family tradition now, but explaining it to my niece and nephew... that's the hardest part."

The fallout from this competition could be significant. According to local resident Jane Doe, who has organized similar events in her community, “It might just bring people together over shared confusion and frustration.” However, Judge Peterson added with a hint of sarcasm, "We’re also considering adding a category for 'most likely to have a heated argument about the film's scientific accuracy.'"

As the fair approaches, organizers are already bracing themselves. "We expect a lot of questions about why we chose this movie," said Fair Director Michael Green. "But hey, it’s better than another year of the same-old pumpkin carving contest."

For those curious about how the competition will unfold, one can only hope that contestants manage to avoid any mention of *The Day After Tomorrow* being a precursor to more scientifically accurate disaster films like *2012*. The judges are reportedly preparing for anything.
