Virtual Reality Conference Goes Offline

Organizers cancel the metaverse hall in favor of fluorescent ballroom panels, citing 'authentic disconnection.

By Helena Cruz · Thursday, May 28, 2026 · SAN FRANCISCO · 4 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

SAN FRANCISCO - The Immersive Future Summit announced Tuesday it will abandon its virtual venue for a downtown hotel ballroom, reversing last year's promise that attendees would never need pants again. Organizer Lisa Nguyen said in-person badges, lukewarm coffee, and awkward hallway small talk deliver "authentic networking you cannot replicate with a headset and a cat filter."

Dr. Jane Kim, a remote-work researcher, called the pivot regressive. "Virtual conferences democratize access," Kim said. "This tells global participants they are welcome if they can afford a Bay Area hotel and tolerate badge lanyards that itch." Nguyen countered that last year's metaverse expo suffered technical glitches, including a keynote speaker trapped inside a loading screen for eleven minutes and a sponsor booth that accidentally sold NFTs of the emergency exit map.

Ticket holders will receive refunds on unused avatar skins and a coupon for one real-world handshake, subject to availability. Sponsors were reassigned to physical banner stands that block fire exits in the traditional manner. Analysts said the move reflects broader industry fatigue with virtual events that promised infinite scale but delivered chat rooms where every question was "Can you hear me now?" Early registration for the ballroom edition sold out in hours, suggesting demand for novelty remains strong when novelty includes pastry trays.

Local color abounded at the press conference. One attendee, a tech enthusiast named Alex, said, "I'm here because I want to see if they can still manage to make networking awkward in person." Another participant, Sarah, added, "At least with VR, you could pretend to be anywhere else but San Francisco."

Nguyen closed the press call by asking attendees to silence phones and souls before entering plenary. She emphasized that this was a serious business event, despite the hotel's proximity to tech startups and venture capital firms. "We want to ensure everyone feels comfortable," Nguyen said, though she quickly added, "Comfort is relative in San Francisco."

The conference organizers also faced criticism from environmentalists who pointed out the carbon footprint of traveling for an event that could have been held virtually. A representative from GreenTech Alliance stated, "While we understand the need for face-to-face interaction, it's disappointing to see such a large-scale tech event revert back to traditional methods." Nguyen responded by saying, "We're aware of the concerns and are working on ways to minimize our impact."

Despite the backlash, some industry insiders saw the move as a strategic decision. Tech analyst Mark Davis said, "Virtual events were a fad. They worked well for quick updates or small groups but struggled with large-scale engagement. This is a smart pivot back to what we know works." However, he added, "It's a bit of a letdown after all the promises of the metaverse."

As attendees began trickling into the hotel ballroom, they were greeted by a mix of excitement and skepticism. One attendee, Tom, said, "I'm here because I heard the coffee is decent. Last year, it was just catnip in a cup." Another participant, Jane, added, "At least now we can actually see each other's faces instead of avatars."

The opening keynote featured a panel discussion on the future of work and collaboration. Despite the high-profile speakers, the session felt somewhat underwhelming compared to last year’s virtual event, which included interactive workshops and immersive experiences. One participant commented, "It's nice to have some real coffee, but I miss the holograms."

As the conference progressed, organizers faced additional challenges. The ballroom setup was cramped, with attendees elbowing each other for space during networking sessions. A representative from a sponsor noted, "The physical stands are great, but they're blocking the exits and making it hard to move around." Nguyen responded, "We’re working on that. Safety is our top priority."

By midday, the conference had already exceeded expectations in terms of attendance, with overflow spilling into nearby meeting rooms. However, some attendees expressed disappointment at the lack of innovation compared to last year’s virtual format. One participant said, "It's nice to be back, but I was hoping for something more... groundbreaking."

As the day drew to a close, Nguyen addressed the crowd, saying, "We understand your concerns and are committed to making this event as successful as possible." She concluded with a dry joke: "Remember, if you see someone wearing a VR headset in the hallway, they're probably just lost. Or maybe they’re still trying to find their way out of the loading screen."

The conference ended on a somewhat somber note, with attendees reflecting on the evolution - and perhaps regression - of technology and its impact on business events. As one participant left the ballroom, they muttered under their breath, "At least we didn't have to wear pants."
