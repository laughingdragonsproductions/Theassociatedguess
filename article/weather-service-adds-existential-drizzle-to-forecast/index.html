Weather Service Adds 'Existential Drizzle' to Forecast

New category describes light rain that makes you question career choices without ruining shoes.

By James Holloway · Monday, May 18, 2026 · 1 min read

CHICAGO — The National Weather Service introduced existential drizzle to its official taxonomy Monday, defining it as "precipitation insufficient to cancel plans but sufficient to suggest plans were never worth having."

Meteorologist Lila Gant said the category fills a gap between mist and melancholy. "People were checking three apps and still not knowing whether to bring an umbrella or a new personality," she said.

The forecast icon—a small cloud staring into the middle distance—already appears across the Midwest. Commuters report arriving damp in spirit if not in body. Baristas say orders of black coffee rose 9% on the first day of classification.

Psychologists cautiously support the change. "Naming the vibe helps," said Dr. Priya Nand. "Clients used to say 'I feel weird.' Now they say 'existential drizzle,' and we can move on."

The Chamber of Tourism opposed the term, requesting rebranding to "gentle motivational moisture." The NWS declined, noting that accuracy remains the agency's core mission, "even when accuracy hurts."

Extended outlook: continued existential drizzle through Thursday, clearing Friday into aggressively sunny denial.
