Local Dog Elected Honorary Mayor After Attending Every Council Meeting

Biscuit campaigned on belly rubs, fiscal responsibility, and not eating the minutes.

By Claire Undertow · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · 1 min read

ASHTON - Town council proclaimed Biscuit, a four-year-old golden retriever, honorary mayor for life Tuesday after records showed he attended 38 consecutive meetings, never interrupted except to sneeze, and lobbied effectively for expanded dog park hours by placing his head on officials' knees.

"Biscuit listens more than some humans we've elected," said Clerk Lila Gant, who swore him in using a treat instead of a Bible.

The dog's platform included pothole awareness ("he feels every one"), transparent garbage-can policy, and mandatory water bowls at outdoor events. Fiscal hawks noted Biscuit has never proposed a tax increase, though he did once eat a proposed budget appendix.

Opposition candidate Hal Voss, a cat, declined to debate, which pollsters said cost him independent voters.

Ceremonial duties include ribbon chewings, parade leading at a leisurely pace, and vetoing proposals that smell like squirrels. Actual governance remains with humans, who consult Biscuit by presenting documents at nose level.

State law unclear on canine officeholders. Attorney general issued guidance that honorary titles are fine "as long as someone scoops the lawn outside town hall."

Residents report improved civic engagement. Meeting attendance rose 40%, mostly among children and people who want to pet the mayor.

Biscuit's first executive order: more tennis balls. Council approved unanimously.
