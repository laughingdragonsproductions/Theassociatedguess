Peter Cullen Dies at 85

The Associated Guess remembers the voice actor who made a generation believe that courage sounded like someone who would stay until the fight was over.

By The Associated Guess Staff · Thursday, August 27, 2026 · 2 min read

LOS ANGELES - Peter Cullen died Tuesday at his home here. He was 85.

Cullen passed away on the night of August 26, his family said. He died peacefully, surrounded by loved ones. No foul play is suspected. For fans who grew up on Saturday mornings, movie-theater matinees, and the particular comfort of a voice that never sounded rushed or cruel, the news landed like a room waiting for a leader who always knew what to say next.

He was born Peter Claver Cullen in Montreal and built a career out of sounds other performers might have treated as background. Optimus Prime in Transformers was the role that followed him across decades, from the original animated series through live-action films and every revival in between. He also voiced Eeyore in Winnie the Pooh, Monterey Jack in Chip 'n Dale: Rescue Rangers, and the unsettling vocalizations of the Predator. Each performance shared the same quality: weight. When Cullen spoke, even as a stuffed donkey or an alien hunter, you believed someone was thinking before the words arrived.

That was the gift. He did not play heroes as though heroism were a costume. He played them as though it were a responsibility.

Cullen often said he found Optimus Prime's steadiness in his older brother Larry, a Marine. Not the volume. Not the machinery. The part where a leader protects people who cannot protect themselves. That is why a generation heard "Autobots, roll out" and felt summoned instead of scolded. That is why "Freedom is the right of all sentient beings" sounded less like a catchphrase and more like a promise someone intended to keep.

We will not pretend this page is neutral. The Associated Guess spends most days inventing nonsense with a straight face. Today we are simply telling the truth: Peter Cullen made kindness sound strong. He made strength sound patient. He made a robot truck feel like the adult in the room when the world felt too loud.

His family asked mourners to honor his legacy by serving their communities with compassion, integrity, and loyalty, and to remember, in his words, to be strong enough to be gentle. Memorial plans remain private. Organizers have asked guests to arrive on time, speak kindly, and refrain from shouting roll-out commands in the parking structure, which several fans acknowledged was fair.

He is gone at 85, on a week when the world already knows what it is like to lose voices that raised us.

So we will carry him instead.

We will never forget the calm in the middle of the battle. We will never forget the brother who lent a Marine's steadiness to a cartoon commander. We will never forget that some people never need to show their face to become unforgettable.

Rest well, Mr. Cullen. Till all are one.
