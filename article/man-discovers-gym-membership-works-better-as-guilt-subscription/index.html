Man Discovers Gym Membership Works Better as Guilt Subscription

Annual fee provides 'ambient fitness energy' without requiring visit.

By Claire Undertow · Sunday, August 9, 2026 · 1 min read

DENVER — Area man Derek Platt reported Tuesday that his gym membership functions optimally as a monthly reminder that he could be healthier, a business model he pays $49 to maintain.

"I visit twice a year," Platt said. "The other months I carry the card in my wallet like a talisman against sedentary shame."

Fitness chain IronPath said many members use facilities primarily as "aspirational infrastructure." Corporate offers a Guilt Plus tier with app notifications that say You Still Good? instead of aggressive coaching.

Behavioral economist Dr. Amir Qasim said guilt subscriptions thrive because canceling feels like admitting defeat. "People keep paying to keep the possibility alive," Qasim said. "It's Netflix for lats."

Platt attempted cancellation once, then rejoined during a January promo because "new year, new me, same credit card."

IronPath piloted a honesty program allowing members to downgrade to Remote Vibes Only for half price. Uptake surprised executives, mostly because people enjoy telling the truth if cheaper.

Platt plans to visit tomorrow, or Thursday, or when the guilt peaks above ambient levels.

Until then, he walks to the mailbox and counts it as cardio.
