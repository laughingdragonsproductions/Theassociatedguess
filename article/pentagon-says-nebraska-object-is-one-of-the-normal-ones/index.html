Pentagon Says Nebraska Object Is One of the Normal Ones

Defense officials classify the cornfield visitor as routine unexplained phenomena, a category that has grown crowded this decade.

By Jordan Pike · Friday, February 6, 2026 · LINCOLN · 3 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

LINCOLN - The Pentagon confirmed Thursday that a luminous object observed hovering above a Nebraska cornfield belongs to a growing inventory of aerial phenomena the Department of Defense now files under "normal, albeit unusual." Press secretary Col. Renee Holt said the object displayed no hostile intent, no discernible propulsion, and no interest in the state fair schedule. "It is one of the normal ones," Holt told reporters. "We have a spreadsheet."

Farmers near the site reported a low hum compatible with irrigation equipment and existential dread. Witness Dale Crisp said the object remained stationary for six hours, then departed "like someone remembered a meeting." "I was out there milking cows when I first spotted it," Crisp recalled. "It just hung in the sky like a giant lightbulb, but not moving. Then, after an hour or so, it took off as if it had to get somewhere."

The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office reviewed radar, satellite, and doorbell camera footage before concluding the event fit pattern 7-B: rural, brief, and inconvenient to explain on cable news without commercial breaks. "We've seen this one a few times," said Col. Holt. "It's like a flying light show that doesn't want to stick around long enough for us to get a good look."

Nebraska Governor Pat Morrow requested federal assistance for tourism marketing. "If it's normal, people will want to see it," Morrow said. "We have corn mazes. We can work with this." Tourism officials are already brainstorming slogans: "See the Normal Glow in Nebraska" and "Why Go Elsewhere When You Can See the Routine?"

UFO enthusiasts criticized the classification as bureaucratic deflation. Researcher Nina Cho argued that labeling mysteries "normal" is how institutions "turn wonder into paperwork." "It's like they're saying, 'Don't worry, it's just a light show, so let's put it in a spreadsheet and forget about it,' " she said.

Holt declined to release the spreadsheet but noted the normal category includes objects that blink, objects that do not blink, and objects that blink only when asked about funding. "It’s like they're trying to make us believe it's just another day in the life of the Pentagon," Cho added. "But we know better."

Local authorities reopened the field after soil samples revealed elevated levels of potassium and one boot print belonging to no living person. "We found this weird footprint near the cornfield," said Sheriff Tom Hanks, who was on scene. "It's like someone or something walked through the mud, but there’s no one around." Officials are considering whether this is a sign of the object's return or just another anomaly.

The object has not returned since its brief visit. Officials consider that normal too. "We've had weirder things happen in Nebraska," said Col. Holt. "Like when the corn really does grow taller than the sky on a windy night." The lack of further sightings, however, has left many wondering if they were just seeing things - or maybe not.

As the days passed without any repeat visits, farmers and locals began to return to their usual routines. But every now and then, someone would mention spotting a faint glow in the distance or hearing an odd humming noise in the night sky. "It’s like it's playing hide-and-seek with us," said Dale Crisp. "And we’re just normal Nebraskans trying to keep our farms going."

The state continues to monitor the situation, keeping a spreadsheet of all sightings and categorizing them as "normal" for now. But who knows what might happen next? As Col. Holt put it, "We'll see if this one sticks around long enough to make a real pattern."
