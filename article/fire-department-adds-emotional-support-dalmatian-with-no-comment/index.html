Fire Department Adds Emotional Support Dalmatian With No Comment

Spot attends blazes solely to look concerned and accept pets from bystanders.

By Claire Undertow · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · PHOENIX · 2 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

PHOENIX - Station 14 introduced Spot, an emotional support dalmatian who does not fight fires, operate hoses, or offer tactical advice, but who is very present emotionally. The fire department’s decision to add a canine with such unique qualifications has sparked both curiosity and skepticism among the community.

"Spot's job is to absorb panic and redistribute it as tail wags," said Captain Dana Hooper, adding that his primary function is to keep media away from hot zones by being photogenic. "He's like the fire department’s mascot, but with a calming presence."

The dalmatian completed certification in standing slightly to the left of danger and making eye contact with worried neighbors. While he may not be trained in CPR, Spot excels in CPE - Canine Public Empathy. Critics questioned the budget allocation until data showed reduced crowd interference at scenes. Bystanders who might otherwise follow firefighters now flock to a shaded chair where they can discuss their day with Spot.

"Since Spot's arrival, we've seen a significant drop in the number of people trying to get close to the action," said Deputy Chief Lisa Martinez. "They just want to sit and talk about their cats or last night’s dinner."

Spot's vest includes patches for Good Boy and Not OSHA But Helpful. He rides in the fire truck second row, windows down, ears authoritative. The department has capitalized on his popularity by selling merchandise that features Spot. Calendar proceeds have exceeded expectations, mostly from people who "just needed something wholesome after the news," according to Hooper.

"The money goes towards equipment and training for our firefighters," said Hooper. "It’s a win-win situation."

Merchandise sales have been brisk, with items ranging from plush toys to t-shirts emblazoned with Spot's image. The department even held a special event where people could meet the dalmatian in person. Attendance was so high that they had to extend the event by an hour.

When asked for comment, Spot licked a reporter’s notebook and was promptly promoted to the role of official spokesperson. "He’s always ready with a wag or a lick," said Hooper. "And he never chases sirens into leadership meetings."

So far, Spot has attended two press conferences and stolen zero microphones. His presence seems to have calmed the public's nerves, making it easier for firefighters to do their job without being interrupted by anxious onlookers.

"Spot’s here to make sure everyone feels a little better," said Hooper with a wry smile. "And if he can’t do that, well, at least he looks good doing it."

In the end, Spot's role may be more about providing comfort than actual firefighting skills. But for now, the fire department seems content to let him continue his duties as an emotional support dalmatian, with no plans to retire him anytime soon.
