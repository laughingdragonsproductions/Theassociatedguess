Fire Department Adds Emotional Support Dalmatian With No Comment

Spot attends blazes solely to look concerned and accept pets from bystanders.

By Claire Undertow · Thursday, July 30, 2026 · 1 min read

PHOENIX — Station 14 introduced Spot, an emotional support dalmatian who does not fight fires, operate hoses, or offer tactical advice, but who is very present emotionally.

"Spot's job is to absorb panic and redistribute it as tail wags," said Captain Dana Hooper. "Also he keeps media away from hot zones by being photogenic."

Spot completed certification in standing slightly to the left of danger and making eye contact with worried neighbors. He is not trained in CPR but excels in CPE—Canine Public Empathy.

Critics questioned budget allocation until data showed reduced crowd interference at scenes. Bystanders who might follow firefighters instead follow Spot to a shaded chair and discuss their day.

Spot's vest includes patches for Good Boy and Not OSHA But Helpful. He rides in the truck second row, windows down, ears authoritative.

Merchandise sales fund equipment. Calendar proceeds exceeded expectations, mostly from people who "just needed something wholesome after the news."

Hooper said expansion depends on Spot not chasing sirens into leadership meetings. So far he has attended two press conferences and stolen zero microphones.

When asked for comment, Spot licked a reporter's notebook and was promoted.
