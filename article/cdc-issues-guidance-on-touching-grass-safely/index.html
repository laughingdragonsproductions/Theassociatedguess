CDC Issues Guidance on Touching Grass Safely

Officials recommend short sessions, sunscreen, and telling someone where you are going emotionally.

By Dr. Helen Marsh · Wednesday, June 24, 2026 · 1 min read

ATLANTA — The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention released interim guidance Thursday for Americans considering touching grass, a wellness trend popularized online as an antidote to chronically online behavior.

Recommendations include limiting initial exposure to five minutes, wearing shoes to prevent surprise moisture, and avoiding grass during peak pollen, peak heat, and peak group chat drama you are trying to escape.

"Start low, go slow," said Dr. Renata Cole. "Some individuals experience side effects such as remembering they own a frisbee or thinking about an ex."

The guidance warns against comparing your grass to influencers' grass, which may be professionally mowed and emotionally curated. If dizziness occurs upon realizing the sky is big, sit down and hydrate.

Employers asked whether grass breaks qualify as PTO. CDC said no, but noted "stepping outside is still cheaper than CalmScroll."

Communities with limited green space may substitute a potted plant and honest window light. Rural residents are advised not to mock urban peers; "they are trying," Cole said.

Follow-up studies will examine whether grass touching reduces urge to post about touching grass. Early data suggests paradoxical posting remains endemic.
