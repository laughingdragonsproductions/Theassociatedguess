College Adds Major in Explaining Memes to Parents

Degree track covers tone, context, and when not to show them anything.

By Dr. Helen Marsh · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · 1 min read

ANN ARBOR - The University of Michigan will offer a B.A. in Intergenerational Memetics starting spring semester, preparing graduates to translate internet humor for relatives who still print MapQuest directions.

Dean Stephen Voss said employer demand drove the curriculum. "Every company needs someone who can tell the CEO why the post is funny without getting HR involved," Voss said.

Core courses include Context Collapse 101, When to Lie and Say You Saw It Live, and Advanced Group Chat Exit Strategies. Capstone project requires successfully explaining a trending video to a panel of actors portraying concerned aunts.

Parents questioned ROI. Voss cited starting salaries at media firms, political campaigns, and any office with Slack.

Scholarships available for first-generation meme explainers from households that still forward chain emails.

Student Owen Kessler enrolled immediately. "My mom thinks LOL means lots of love," Kessler said. "This is a calling."

Accreditation pending review by a board that requested the syllabus be sent as a PDF, not a TikTok.

Graduates receive a diploma and a laminated card reading Just Show Them the Dog Video Instead.
