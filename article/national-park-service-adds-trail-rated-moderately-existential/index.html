National Park Service Adds Trail Rated 'Moderately Existential

New signage warns hikers they may arrive at the summit questioning every life choice except bringing water.

By James Holloway · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · 1 min read

MOAB - The National Park Service unveiled updated trail ratings Friday, adding moderately existential to its difficulty scale between strenuous and why did I think this was a good idea.

Ranger Lila Gant said the label reflects terrain that is physically manageable but spiritually ambiguous. "You gain 800 feet and lose a relationship with certainty," Gant said.

Signs advise carrying two liters of water, sun protection, and "a willingness to sit on a rock and stare until something resolves or doesn't."

Early visitors praised accuracy. "The incline was fine," said hiker Owen Kessler. "The part where I remembered I never learned guitar - that was the trail."

Search and rescue reported no increase in emergencies, though counselors noted a rise in visitors saying they needed to "figure some stuff out" while clearly talking about more than elevation.

The park gift shop now sells shirts reading I Survived Moderately Existential and Came Back Anyway. Proceeds fund trail maintenance and a quiet bench program.

Gant reminded the public that all wilderness carries risk, including the risk of clarity. "If you wanted easy answers," she said, "you should have stayed in the parking lot with the gourds."
