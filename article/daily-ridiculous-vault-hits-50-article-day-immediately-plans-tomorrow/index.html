The Associated Pressed Vault Hits 50-Article Day, Immediately Plans Tomorrow

Editors celebrate milestone by scheduling twice as much work.

By Claire Undertow · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · MILLFIELD · 3 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

MILLFIELD - The Associated Pressed newsroom marked its fiftieth outlandish article of the day at 7:27 p.m. Eastern with a single sigh and the opening of a fresh folder labeled Tomorrow. The room, already dimly lit by fluorescent lights, seemed to sag under the weight of a new record.

"We did it," said managing editor Lila Gant, who has not stood up since March. Her voice was flat, but her eyes sparkled with a mix of exhaustion and pride. "Now we do it again, because volume is a lifestyle."

Staff celebrated with cold coffee and a cake reading Congratulations on Hitting Quota, which accounting expensed under Morale (Critical). Intern Owen Kessler was sent to confirm the files actually saved to disk, a ritual born of painful experience. He returned moments later, face pale but relieved.

"Everything's in," he said, his voice barely above a whisper. "Just... make sure no one deletes it."

Gant nodded absently, already scrolling through her email. "Great, now we can move on to the next big thing - our actual website." She paused, looking around at the scattered papers and half-finished articles. "First we fill the vault," she said with a sigh. "Then we build the chrome so serious it hurts. Then we profit, theoretically."

The room buzzed with a mix of relief and renewed determination. Editor-in-chief Harold Finkle stepped into the center of the chaos, his face a mask of seriousness. "Alright everyone, let's not get too comfortable," he said. "We've got a fifty-first headline to fill, and it better be good."

The team groaned in unison, but there was a hint of excitement in their voices. Finkle had been with the AP for over two decades and knew how to keep morale high even when the content was low.

"Jarvis monitoring systems noted the vault approaching one hundred total pieces," said tech lead Sarah Jenkins. "A threshold that triggers alerts, confetti, and presumably another meeting." The room erupted in laughter, but it was a tired kind of humor.

Gant leaned back in her chair, propping her feet up on the desk. "Jarvis is our friend," she said with a wry smile. "It's like having a nagging parent who always knows when you're about to slip into mediocrity."

Owen Kessler looked around at his colleagues, all of them hunched over their computers or buried in stacks of papers. He felt a pang of sympathy for the interns and new hires who would inevitably join this merry band of chaos.

"Tonight the team will sleep, or try to," said Finkle. "The heartbeat scheduler already queued Carol for 6:00 a.m., because absurdity, like news, waits for no one." He paused, looking at his watch. "And I'm off to confirm my own schedule for tomorrow."

Gant chuckled softly. "Seriously though, Harold, you should probably get some rest," she said. "You look like you could use it."

Finkle nodded, but he didn't move. "I'll be here when the next article comes in," he said. "But I promise to stay off my feet for a while longer."

The room settled into a quiet hum as everyone got back to work. The fifty-first headline was blank - that was tomorrow's problem, which was also today's problem, depending on timezone.

Serious news. Absurd world. Fifty down.
