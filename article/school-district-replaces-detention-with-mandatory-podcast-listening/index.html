School District Replaces Detention With Mandatory Podcast Listening

Officials say fifteen minutes of true crime 'builds empathy and better headphones hygiene.

By Claire Undertow · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · 1 min read

MAPLE RIDGE - Starting this fall, students who disrupt class will serve Podcast Reflection instead of detention, a program superintendent Dana Hooper described as "restorative, educational, and slightly terrifying in a wholesome way."

"Writing 'I will not throw grapes' fifty times didn't work," Hooper said. "But hearing a calm narrator describe forensic accounting? That builds character."

Offenders receive noise-canceling earbuds and a curated playlist ranging from etiquette experts to episodes about people who lied on resumes and got caught at the company picnic. Repeat incidents escalate to documentary series about bridge maintenance, which staff call "the nuclear option."

Parents were split. "My son finally learned what a Roth IRA is," said Elena Platt. Others worried about age-appropriate content. The district promised filters, though IT director Gil Marr admitted "one kid already found a show about competitive cheese."

Teachers report improved hallway behavior and a 22% increase in students saying "interesting" without meaning it. Critics note the policy outsources discipline to advertisers.

Hooper said traditional detention may return if podcast hosts unionize. Until then, the district has ordered forty charging stations and one therapist who specializes in parasocial relationships.
