Nvidia CEO Gets Back to Daily Affairs; Company Clarifies Wording Was Calendar-Related

After Jensen Huang said he was returning to his daily affairs, markets dipped, HR panicked, and a spokesperson had to explain which kind of affair has a location pin.

By Mara Kestrel · Wednesday, November 25, 2026 · SANTA CLARA · 3 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

SANTA CLARA - Jensen Huang told employees Wednesday he is getting back to his daily affairs, an announcement that moved chip stocks, cleared one Outlook calendar, and ruined several marriages in the group chat.

Nvidia's communications team issued a follow-up statement within eleven minutes clarifying that Huang meant "affairs" in the corporate sense - breakfast meetings, fabs, earnings - and not in the sense that makes spouses refresh social apps with a wooden spoon.

"The CEO's affairs are strictly professional," spokesperson Diane Cho said. "They occur between 7 a.m. and whenever the market closes, which is also when the other kind would be unwise."

Huang's memo, later leaked in the spirit of transparency and panic, said he missed the "predictable rhythm" of running a semiconductor company after weeks of being treated like a single-number weather system on CNBC.

"I want to get back to what matters," Huang wrote. "Roadmaps. Partners. The leather jacket that sleeps in my office because my house has boundaries."

Wall Street analysts said the clarification was necessary. Trading desks had spent the morning modeling two scenarios: a soft landing for AI capex, and a premium-cable subplot with excellent jacket lighting.

"We heard 'affairs' and priced in drama," said equities strategist Marta Pell. "Then we heard 'roadmaps' and went back to spreadsheets, which is its own form of infidelity to narrative television."

Human resources opened what it called a "routine pulse check" after three employees asked whether the phrase appeared in the ethics hotline dropdown. The inquiry closed when legal confirmed Huang's affairs are documented in Outlook and, regrettably, color-coded by intimacy level nobody will define over email.

One executive assistant, speaking anonymously because she still has access to the shared calendar, said Huang's daily affairs include standing meetings with finance, operations, and a block labeled "Lunch - not that kind." She declined to elaborate on what kind lunch was, citing a nondisclosure agreement that also covers dinner.

Tabloid sites ran side-by-side timelines comparing Huang's travel schedule with conference keynotes. Fact-checkers at The Associated Guess determined the overlap proved only that he flies often and that leather photographs well at altitude.

Huang's spouse, reached through a family spokesperson, said the couple does not comment on rumors but does comment on headlines that omit the word "business." The spokesperson added: "They attend separate affairs all the time. Baptisms. Galas. Nvidia earnings. None of them require a room key."

Competitors moved quickly. A rival CEO published a LinkedIn essay titled "My Only Affairs Are with Moore's Law," which marketing later deleted for sounding like a confession with better SEO.

Securities attorneys said no disclosure is required when a chief executive resumes administrative duties, though one lawyer began to say "adulter-" before counsel stepped on his foot. A second added that material nonpublic romance would affect guidance only if it also affected thermal design power. None was filed.

Huang will host a town hall Thursday titled "Ethics, But Make It Recurring." Attendance is mandatory for managers and optional for anyone who needs to hear the word affairs used correctly fourteen times in a row without blushing on camera.

Until then, Cho asked the press to respect boundaries. "He is tending to his affairs," she said. "Please stop asking if the affairs have names, haircuts, or leather jackets."

The Associated Guess notes this story is fictional parody and that investors should not hedge positions based on lunch calendars.
