Congress Discovers Future Already Happened, Schedules Hearing

Lawmakers demand answers about timeline overlap after staffers find tomorrow's minutes in today's binder.

By James Holloway · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · 1 min read

WASHINGTON - The House Select Committee on Temporal Oversight convened an emergency session Wednesday after a junior aide discovered that minutes for a hearing scheduled for next Thursday had already been filed, stamped, and summarized on cable news.

"We are not alleging time travel," said Chair Rep. Lila Gant. "We are alleging the future happened early, which, if true, makes most of our fundraising emails obsolete."

The binder in question includes a vote tally on a bill that has not been written, a quote attributed to a senator who was in Nebraska, and a catering invoice for chicken that "tastes like regret," according to a footnote no one remembers adding.

Minority staffers argued the document was a draft. Majority staffers countered that drafts do not include C-SPAN timestamps. The Capitol physician was summoned after three members reported déjà vu severe enough to qualify for hazard pay.

Physicists consulted by the committee offered little comfort. "Linear time is a social construct," said Dr. Stephen Voss of the Institute for Obvious Explanations. "Also someone left a Post-it that says 'you already knew.'"

The committee voted 9–4 to hold a hearing about the hearing, then realized that vote also appeared in the binder on page twelve. Parliamentarian Eleanor Peck ruled the situation "procedurally moist" and suggested everyone go home until causality "sorts itself out."

No legislation is expected. A spokesperson for the Senate said upper chamber leaders prefer to wait until the future happens on their schedule, "preferably after recess."

Witnesses will include the aide, the printer, and a clock from the Rayburn bathroom that has been fast since 1987.
