Economists Declare Soft Landing Has Been Replaced by Soft Ambivalence

Markets rise, fall, and then check phone for other news.

By Marcus Tweed · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · NEW YORK · 1 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

NEW YORK - Leading economists announced Tuesday that the business cycle has entered soft ambivalence, a phase characterized by moderate growth, moderate inflation, and a collective shrug measurable on consumer sentiment surveys.

"We used to panic elegantly," said Dr. Amir Qasim. "Now we panic, then refresh, then order salad."

The Federal Reserve held rates steady while issuing a statement longer than anyone read, which markets interpreted as hawkish, dovish, and "maybe about birds."

Corporate earnings calls featured executives praising resilience while quietly hiring consultants to explain why resilience feels exhausting. Analysts upgraded several stocks to Hold But Emotionally We Get It.

Retail spending remains strong on goods that solve problems invented last month, including desk treadmills and lamps that simulate sunset because real sunset requires closing the laptop.

Policy makers debated stimulus versus restraint at a luncheon where everyone agreed the chicken was fine. No conclusions were reached, aligning with the theme.

Qasim predicted soft ambivalence could persist until something unmistakable happens, such as an election, a hurricane, or someone finally cleaning the fridge.

Until then, investors are advised to diversify into cash, bonds, and hobbies that do not track CPI.
