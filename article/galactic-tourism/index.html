Galactic Tourism Takes Off

Weekend packages to the moon now include luggage that weighs more emotionally than physically.

By Brandon Sparks · Thursday, April 30, 2026 · HOUSTON · 1 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

HOUSTON - Galactic Excursions LLC sold out its first quarter of "Near Space Weekends," offering tourists a suborbital selfie, a meal labeled astronaut-adjacent, and a gift shop magnet that demagnetizes mysteriously.

CEO Renata Cho said demand exceeded projections after influencers described Earth as "small, blue, and extremely reviewable." Packages start at $42,000, or three years of ignoring credit card statements.

Travel agencies added moon add-ons, Mars waitlists, and a premium tier called LEGEND that mostly includes a hat. Environmental groups asked about rocket emissions; Cho said rockets emit "wonder" and promised a PDF.

Passenger Wade Ellison returned Monday calling the trip life-changing. "You see the curve of the planet and realize your inbox is not that important," Ellison said, while checking his inbox.

Regulators drafted rules for space tourism liability, lost luggage beyond the Kármán line, and whether turbulence includes existential dread. Insurance companies introduced riders for "sudden perspective."

Local economies near launch sites reported booming hotel sales to families who wave at relatives through a fence and pretend they can see anything.

Critics call galactic tourism a vanity project for the wealthy. Cho countered that vanity is a renewable resource and cheaper than fixing airports.

Next month Galactic Excursions launches "Honeymoon Crater," marketed to couples who want their love to feel interstellar and their photos to require ND filters.

Earth remains available for day trips at ground level while supplies last.
