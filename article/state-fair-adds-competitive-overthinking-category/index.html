State Fair Adds Competitive Overthinking Category

Judges score participants on loops per minute and quality of imaginary arguments in the shower.

By Claire Undertow · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · 1 min read

DES MOINES - The Iowa State Fair introduced competitive overthinking to its talent lineup this year, drawing 140 entrants who demonstrated advanced rumination across text-message subtext, career hypotheticals, and whether they sounded weird saying goodbye on a Zoom call.

Judges evaluated technical difficulty, emotional spillover, and "did you bring up something from 2016 unprompted." Gold medalist Renee Kwan scored 9.4 on a routine about an email she has not sent since March.

"She maintained eye contact with a middle distance that suggested profound inner litigation," said head judge Dr. Priya Nand.

Event organizers installed quiet zones and hydration stations labeled Maybe You're Hungry, Not Broken. Sponsors included a meditation app and a mattress company that understands 2 a.m.

Traditionalists complained the fair is losing focus on livestock. Fair president Gil Marr noted that cows also overthink, "mostly about gates," and said all species deserve recognition.

Youth division winners received ribbons and a pamphlet on cognitive reframing. Adults received gift cards to therapy, or coffee, "depending on what they overthink about therapy."

Next year organizers plan a team event: group chat analysis relay.
