State Fair Adds Competitive Overthinking Category

Judges score participants on loops per minute and quality of imaginary arguments in the shower.

By Claire Undertow · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · DES MOINES · 3 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

DES MOINES - The Iowa State Fair introduced competitive overthinking to its talent lineup this year, drawing 140 entrants who demonstrated advanced rumination across text-message subtext, career hypotheticals, and whether they sounded weird saying goodbye on a Zoom call.

Judges evaluated technical difficulty, emotional spillover, and "did you bring up something from 2016 unprompted." Gold medalist Renee Kwan scored 9.4 on a routine about an email she has not sent since March. "She maintained eye contact with a middle distance that suggested profound inner litigation," said head judge Dr. Priya Nand, who added, "We were impressed by her ability to make the audience question their own decision-making processes."

Event organizers installed quiet zones and hydration stations labeled Maybe You're Hungry, Not Broken. Sponsors included a meditation app called Mindful Moments and a mattress company that understands 2 a.m., which offered special discounts for fairgoers who could explain why they needed an extra-long bed.

Traditionalists complained the fair was losing focus on livestock. Fair president Gil Marr noted that cows also overthink, "mostly about gates," and said all species deserve recognition. "We're not saying cows should compete with humans in overthinking," he added, "but it's good to see diversity."

Youth division winners received ribbons and a pamphlet on cognitive reframing. Adults received gift cards to therapy, or coffee, "depending on what they overthink about therapy." One attendee who received both commented, "I think I'll go for the therapy first because if I'm going to spend money on this, I want it to be effective."

Local color abounded at the fair. A group of teenagers from Des Moines High School performed a skit where one student tried to decide what to wear to a party, ultimately choosing between a plaid shirt and a button-down, only to realize they had forgotten about their best friend's birthday. The judges awarded them a perfect score for "unintentional authenticity."

Event organizers also hosted workshops on how to manage overthinking in daily life. One workshop leader, Dr. Raj Patel, suggested keeping a journal of overthought scenarios and then writing down the opposite scenario to balance it out. "It’s about finding that inner equilibrium," he said.

Next year's organizers plan a team event: group chat analysis relay. Participants will be divided into teams and given a series of text messages to analyze for hidden meanings, with points awarded based on depth of interpretation. Security reported zero fights in the overthinking tent but several prolonged staring contests that required gentle escorts to the funnel cake line.

In an unexpected twist, a local farmer named Harold Johnson entered the competition as well. He explained his motivation: "I just wanted to see if I could outthink my cows." His routine involved a detailed analysis of why he should or shouldn't buy more land for his farm, leading judges to award him a bronze medal.

As the fair wound down, attendees mused on their own overthinking habits and whether they had benefited from the experience. One woman commented, "I feel like I've spent the whole day in my head, but it was kind of nice to see other people doing the same thing."

The Iowa State Fair's new category of competitive overthinking proved both entertaining and thought-provoking, leaving many attendees with a newfound appreciation for the complexity of their own minds.
