Digital Sushi Makes a Splash

AR tasting menus let diners enjoy fish that never swam and guilt that feels surprisingly real.

By Riley Dunn · Thursday, March 19, 2026 · SEATTLE · 1 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

SEATTLE - Chef Aiko Tan launched Pixel Nigiri, a restaurant where diners wear headsets and haptic chopsticks to "eat" sushi rendered in 8K while a real bowl of rice sits sadly in front of them for legal reasons.

Tan said digital sushi solves overfishing, shipping costs, and the awkwardness of mispronouncing omakase at dates. Critics asked whether food requires calories. Tan offered a loyalty program instead.

Early reviews praise vivid texture and zero mercury, noting the tuna tastes like ambition and the salmon tastes like a software update. Traditionalists held a candlelight vigil at a harbor that still smells like the sea.

Health inspectors classified Pixel Nigiri as entertainment with rice. Tan framed that as Michelin-adjacent wordplay.

Food bloggers streamed live reactions, chewing carefully for cameras while eating air with commitment. One viral clip shows a diner crying because the virtual uni reminded them of vacation.

Supply chain experts call the model disruptive. Fishermen call it Tuesday.

Tan plans seasonal menus tied to firmware: Spring Roll 2.1, Spicy Tuna Patch Notes, Dessert Beta Only On Weekends.

Doctors warn against replacing meals entirely. Tan introduced a supplement bar selling real pickles "for crunch verification."

Investors valued Pixel Nigiri at $200 million, or roughly one very good real sushi restaurant times infinity.

Reservation waitlist is three months. Tan recommends arriving hungry for authenticity and leaving confused for brand recall.

Real fish unavailable by design, which Tan said is the point until the oceans send a press release.
