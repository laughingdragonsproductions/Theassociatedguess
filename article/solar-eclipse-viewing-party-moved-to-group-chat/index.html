Solar Eclipse Viewing Party Moved to Group Chat

Organizers cite 'better attendance when nobody has to drive.

By James Holloway · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · INDIANAPOLIS · 3 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

INDIANAPOLIS - The city’s official eclipse watch relocated from the riverfront to a moderated WhatsApp thread after planners realized turnout improves when participation requires only thumbs. "Parking was a nightmare in 2017," said events coordinator Lila Gant, sipping on a lukewarm coffee. "Typing 'wow' from couch scales."

The virtual party includes timed emoji reactions - smiling sun emojis at the start, frowning cloud faces as the moon covers more of the sun, and finally, a full-blown eclipse face for totality. A pinned link to NASA’s live stream ensures everyone has access to the cosmic event, though some complained about Americans live-posting darkness, which inevitably leads to a strict no-spoilers policy for people in later time zones.

Local astronomy club provided safety tips as a PDF because "nobody reads until the last minute anyway." Gant explained that bots will ping users every five minutes with reminders not to stare at the sun through their phone screens. “Someone always tries,” she said, her voice tinged with exasperation. The chat opens ten minutes before totality, and Gant recommends muting the uncle who forwards conspiracy JPEGs.

Sponsors include an eyewear brand and a therapy app anticipating existential feelings during totality. The eyewear company offers discounts to participants for purchasing eclipse glasses at local libraries, while the therapy app promises immediate relief from any post-eclipse anxiety. Brick-and-mortar vendors protested lost foot traffic, but Gant offered a compromise: pick up your glasses at the library and watch alone, posting photos on the chat for validation.

Meteorologists forecast clear skies and heavy notification volume. "It’s like predicting a snowstorm in Florida," said meteorologist Sarah Thompson. "But we’re confident." Historians note this continues humanity's shift from gathering in fields to gathering in threads, which is either community or performance, depending on your notification settings.

As the day progresses, the chat buzzes with excitement and questions. “How do you pronounce totality?” asks one user. Another wonders if it’s safe to look at the sun through a camera lens. Gant patiently responds, "No, it's not." The city council even passed an emergency resolution declaring the virtual event as official city business for tax purposes.

By 2:30 PM, the chat is abuzz with countdowns and last-minute tips. “Remember to take off your glasses before looking at the sun!” warns a user, their message followed by numerous thumbs-ups. As the moon begins its descent, the chat transforms into a sea of awe-struck emojis - eyes wide open, mouths agape.

The moment of totality arrives, and the chat goes silent for just a few seconds before erupting with reactions. “Did you see that?!” exclaims one user, their message followed by dozens of smiling sun faces. The city’s official eclipse watch may have moved to a group chat, but it seems to have brought everyone closer together - virtually.

As the sun begins its return, Gant smiles, her eyes scanning the screen for any signs of trouble. “We made it,” she says softly, as the chat continues to hum with post-eclipse chatter and plans for next year’s event.
