New Law Requires All Citizens to Vote in the Next Election

Turnout mandate includes ceremonial ballots, sticker distribution, and a firm maybe on whether anyone counts them.

By James Holloway · Thursday, May 28, 2026 · WASHINGTON · 3 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

WASHINGTON - Congress passed the Mandatory Participation Act on Thursday, requiring every eligible citizen to cast a ballot in the next federal election whether or not election officials agree the ballot exists.

Sponsor Rep. Lila Crane said the measure addresses "embarrassing turnout photos" and foreign commentators who ask why democracy looks like a parking lot on a holiday. "Voting is civic duty," Crane said, adding with a wry smile, "We added stickers." The law mandates that every citizen must participate in the electoral process, even if they are not sure where to find their polling place or what candidates are running.

Under the new legislation, nonvoters face a series of escalating reminders: mailers, texts, a voicemail from a local official, and finally a volunteer who knocks on doors offering coffee while describing how hard people fought for the franchise. The volunteers will be equipped with a script that reads, "Did you know that your ancestors marched across deserts in search of the right to vote? Now it's your turn."

Critics note the bill does not require states to count provisional ballots, fix broken machines, or agree on what a district is. Legal scholar Prof. Omar Ndiaye called it "participation theater with excellent stationery." He added, "It’s like requiring people to wear masks without providing them with actual masks."

Election administrators requested funding for extra staff, scanners, and therapy. They received funding for stickers and a PDF titled "Vibes of Civic Engagement." The document includes tips on how to maintain positive energy during the voting process, such as "Smile at strangers" and "Sing your favorite campaign song."

The law exempts citizens who submit a doctor's note, travel proof, or a 300-word essay about how busy they are. Essay readers will be volunteers who also run the bake sale. The bake sale proceeds will go toward funding for the next round of mandatory participation stickers.

State officials in three swing districts announced simultaneous audits of the Mandatory Participation Act, the previous Mandatory Participation Act, and a binder labeled DO NOT OPEN BEFORE NOVEMBER. They expressed concern that the new law might be more symbolic than practical, given the ongoing issues with voter registration databases and polling place accessibility.

Crane urged calm. "Show up, vote, feel proud," she said. "We will figure out the rest after the cameras leave." She added, "And remember to wear your mandatory participation sticker proudly."

Local color from a small town in Iowa revealed that residents were divided on the new law. One resident, Sarah Thompson, said, "I'm all for voting, but this feels like they're forcing us into a box. I just want to vote for who I think is best." Another resident, Tom Johnson, added, "At least it's not mandatory knitting or something."

The law also includes provisions for those who are out of town on election day. These individuals will receive a special absentee ballot that must be returned by midnight the following day. The post office has been instructed to deliver these ballots via drone, in case regular mail is too slow.

In an effort to boost voter turnout, the government has launched a campaign called "Voting is Cool." It features social media influencers and celebrities encouraging their followers to vote. However, some critics argue that this approach might backfire, stating, "If voting was cool, we wouldn't need laws to make people do it."

The law's implementation will be closely monitored by both supporters and detractors alike. As the next election approaches, the nation prepares for a new era of civic engagement - or perhaps just a lot more stickers.
