Scientists Confirm Cats Have Been Ignoring Us on Purpose

Peer-reviewed study finds selective hearing is 'an evolutionary feature, not a bug.

By Dr. Helen Marsh · Thursday, May 28, 2026 · 1 min read

CAMBRIDGE — Researchers at the Institute of Feline Indifference published findings Wednesday confirming that domestic cats possess the cognitive ability to respond to their names and choose not to, a behavior lead author Dr. Stephen Voss called "active disengagement."

"We played recordings of owners saying 'treat' versus 'tax documents,'" Voss said. "Ear twitch latency differed by four hundred milliseconds. They know. They defer."

The study tracked 600 cats across twelve households using collar sensors and hidden cameras. Cats responded fastest to can openers and slowest to phrases including "time for a pill" and "we need to talk."

Animal behaviorists say the results validate what owners suspected since ancient Egypt. "This is not aloofness," said Dr. Priya Nand. "This is queue management."

The Cat Lobby, an industry group representing premium litter brands, welcomed the study and announced a campaign celebrating "mindful boundaries."

Owners expressed relief and betrayal. "I thought the vet's name was too hard," said Owen Kessler, whose cat Pancake responded only when Kessler whispered about salmon at 2 a.m.

Voss said further research will examine whether cats read text messages marked urgent. Early data suggests they do and still wait until convenient.
