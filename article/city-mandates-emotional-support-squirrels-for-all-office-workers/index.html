City Mandates Emotional Support Squirrels for All Office Workers

The Department of Labor says the policy addresses 'unprecedented workplace loneliness' while acknowledging most squirrels remain unclear on HIPAA.

By Marcus Tweed · Tuesday, April 28, 2026 · 1 min read

CEDAR POINT — The city council voted unanimously Thursday to require every employer with more than twelve workers to provide a certified emotional support squirrel by January, a measure officials called "the minimum humane response to three years of hybrid confusion."

"We tried meditation apps," said Labor Commissioner Dana Hooper. "We tried snack walls. The snack walls attracted more squirrels than employees, which is when we realized we were already halfway there."

Under the ordinance, squirrels must complete a forty-hour de-escalation course that includes not running up pant legs during quarterly reviews and not burying important documents in planter boxes. Employers may deduct up to $400 per rodent from taxable income, provided the animal does not chew through the ethernet.

Human resources departments reported mixed results during the pilot. "Chester listens when I talk about my ex-wife," said accounts payable specialist Renee Kwan. "He also stole my lunch and hid it in the ceiling tile, which felt accurate."

The Chamber of Commerce opposed the rule, arguing that squirrels "lack accountability structures" and "will unionize before the interns do." Chamber president Gil Marr pointed to a downtown law firm where a support squirrel allegedly scheduled six meetings by stepping on a Outlook shortcut.

City veterinarians will inspect workplaces monthly for adequate acorn reserves and "a general absence of hawk activity." Violators face fines of $2,500 per unsupervised employee sadness.

When asked whether the policy would extend to remote workers, Hooper said the city was "still determining whether a squirrel over Zoom counts if it is technically in another jurisdiction." A guidance document is expected never, according to the clerk, who was briefly distracted by something in the oak outside.
