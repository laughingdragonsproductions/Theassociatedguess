Area Man's Portfolio Now Emotionally Diversified

Financial advisor recommends balancing regret, cautious hope, and one meme stock 'for the plot.

By Marcus Tweed · Tuesday, May 5, 2026 · 1 min read

NORWALK — After three years of checking his retirement account "just to see if it was still there," area man Owen Kessler announced Tuesday that his portfolio is finally emotionally diversified, a milestone his advisor described as "more stable than the actual numbers."

"I've got 40% in index funds, 30% in bonds, 20% in cash, and 10% in a company I don't understand but liked the logo," Kessler said. "Emotionally, though, I'm spread across mild panic, performative calm, and one position I refuse to discuss at Thanksgiving."

Certified financial planner Lila Gant said emotional diversification protects investors from putting all their anxiety in one basket. "Owen used to refresh his app every four minutes," she said. "Now he refreshes every six, and only on green days, which we call growth."

Kessler's largest holding remains a broad market ETF, which he refers to as "the thing I pretend I chose on purpose." His smallest position is a cryptocurrency named after a dog that died in 2011, which he bought "because a guy on a podcast sounded confident, and I miss having hobbies."

Behavioral economists say many Americans now treat investing less as wealth building and more as a subscription to suspense. "The market opens at nine-thirty and delivers content," said Dr. Priya Nand. "Some people pay Netflix. Owen pays volatility."

Kessler plans to rebalance quarterly, or whenever he reads a headline containing the word "unprecedented." His advisor recommended setting alerts for price targets, not feelings. Kessler said he would think about it after lunch, then checked his phone twice before finishing the sentence.
