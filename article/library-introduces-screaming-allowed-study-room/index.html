Library Introduces 'Screaming Allowed' Study Room

Soundproof booth accommodates finals week, tax season, and 'general life.

By Claire Undertow · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · BOULDER · 3 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

BOULDER - The public library unveiled its first Scream Booth on Tuesday, a soundproof room where patrons may vocalize frustration for up to ten minutes per visit, provided they sign a waiver acknowledging "this is not therapy, though it may feel close." Director Hal Voss said demand followed years of whisper-only policy that left students hissing rage into textbooks. "We saw healthy coping migrate to parking garages," Voss said. "We want literacy and catharsis under one roof."

The booth includes a laminated feelings wheel, a stress ball rated for industrial use, and a timer that flashes gentle blue before cutting power to prevent "extended opera." Security reported one incident involving a graduate student and a thesis chapter titled "Conclusions (Final Final)." The student, who wished to remain anonymous, said, "I was just trying to get it done. It's not like I had any other options."

Usage slots booked solid through October. Waitlist members may scream in a designated outdoor gazebo during approved weather, excluding existential drizzle. One such waitlist member, Emily Jenkins, a local high school student, said she signed up as soon as the program was announced. "I can't believe I'm allowed to let it all out," Jenkins said with a mix of relief and trepidation.

Neighboring businesses had concerns. The yoga studio next door requested thicker walls. The coffee shop reported increased sales of chamomile and whatever sells when people emerge shaking but smiling. Owner Sarah Thompson said, "We're not against letting off steam, but we do prefer our customers to leave feeling a bit more centered."

The library's expansion plans include adding more booths and outdoor spaces for those who cannot wait their turn. Voss said the program will expand if overdue fines decrease. "People return books when they're emotionally regulated," he said. "Also when we turn off Wi-Fi, but we're trying positivity first." He added with a wry smile, "We can't have everyone screaming into their devices."

The city council also weighed in on the matter. Councilwoman Lisa Martinez said, "While I appreciate the library's efforts to address mental health, perhaps they should consider offering some actual therapy sessions instead of just letting people scream at each other." She continued, "But hey, if it keeps the peace and the books coming back, who am I to complain?"

Local color abounds in the form of a group of high school students who gathered outside the library after their first session. One student, Jake Thompson, said, "It was intense but cathartic. I felt like I could finally let go." Another, Sarah Lee, added, "I think it's going to be a hit with everyone who needs a break from the world."

As for the future of the Scream Booth, Voss hinted that they might explore other forms of stress relief. "We're looking into meditation rooms and even a pet therapy area," he said. "But for now, we'll stick with what works." The library's next challenge is to ensure that the booth remains a place of solace rather than just another source of noise complaints.

In the meantime, the library continues to monitor usage and gather feedback from patrons. With the success of the Scream Booth, it seems clear that sometimes, letting out a good scream can be as important as reading a good book.
