Local Bakery Introduces New Ghost Bread

After months of midnight complaints, Hartwell's Crumb & Specter releases a loaf formulated for entities that cannot hold utensils.

By Mae Holloway · Friday, February 27, 2026 · HARTWELL · 1 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

HARTWELL - Hartwell's Crumb & Specter Bakery unveiled Ghost Bread on Thursday, a sourdough variant engineered for patrons who arrive after closing, pass through walls, and have historically left flour handprints on ceilings.

Owner Denise Korr said the recipe began as damage control. "We tried signs. We tried holy water near the register. The ghosts kept eating our day-old rye and leaving reviews that only dogs could hear."

The loaf uses a low-density crumb, trace amounts of ectoplasm-neutral salt, and a crust designed to audibly crunch for beings without corporeal ears. Focus groups of local mediums rated the texture "present" and the aroma "like a memory of wheat."

City health inspectors approved the product under a provisional category for "non-corporeal food service," provided the bakery maintains separate tongs and does not claim the bread cures unfinished business.

Paranormal economist Dr. Lyle Penn noted Hartwell's ghost population has risen 12% since the pandemic, when many residents simply stopped leaving their houses and, according to municipal records, their bodies.

Korr said early sales exceeded projections after a viral video showed a translucent figure in a top hat purchasing four loaves with exact change that materialized from nowhere. Skeptics called it a marketing stunt involving fishing line and a cousin in accounting.

The bakery will offer Ghost Bread Thursdays alongside its regular menu. Gluten-free and vegan options remain unavailable to the deceased, who Korr said "are already on the strictest diet imaginable."

Neighboring businesses welcomed the innovation. The laundromat next door reported fewer floating socks. The funeral home across the street declined comment but extended hours.

Korr invited skeptics to visit after midnight. "Bring appetite or bring salt," she said. "We can work with either."
