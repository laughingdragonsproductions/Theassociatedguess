Trump Unveils Space Force Uniform Pentagon Insists Is Not Imperial

Officials said the gray service dress tunic reflects American discipline and absolutely not a galaxy far, far away where someone named Palpatine made questionable staffing choices.

By Hank Silt · Monday, September 7, 2026 · WASHINGTON · 2 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

WASHINGTON - The White House unveiled a next-generation Space Force service dress uniform Monday that officials described as "modern, authoritative, and unmistakably American," while online commentators described it as "what happens when procurement watches the wrong trilogy."

The design features a space-gray tunic with a high collar, silver shoulder boards, knee-high boots, and a peaked cap bearing the Space Force delta. A briefing slide titled "Design Inspiration" credited the discipline and functionality of Starship Trooper uniforms, then added, in smaller type, "modernized for the Guardians of the Space Domain."

"This is not cosplay," said a senior defense official who asked not to be named because his group chat had already sent seventeen memes. "This is the uniform of a branch that operates above the atmosphere and below the threshold of people saying the quiet part out loud."

President Donald Trump praised the look at a hangar event, calling it "very strong, very tall, very commanding," and noting that several attendees instinctively stood at attention before being reminded they were civilians at a podium, not bridge officers awaiting orders.

Pentagon spokespeople rejected comparisons to Imperial officer attire from Star Wars, noting that those uniforms lacked an official color palette slide listing Space Gray, Midnight Black, and Satin Black. "Our delta is right-side up," one spokesperson said. "That is the difference between service and whatever those other guys were doing."

Guardians who viewed the mock-up asked practical questions, including whether the high collar interferes with headset use, whether the boots are regulation for zero-gravity treadmills, and whether the belt buckle is large enough to reflect sunlight toward enemy satellites. Leadership said answers would arrive in a follow-on memo after someone figures out what a follow-on memo means in space.

Science fiction historians were less convinced. "Whenever a military says the uniform is about discipline, someone nearby is about to explain a superweapon," said Dr. Priya Nand, who does not work for the Pentagon but has seen movies.

The Space Force said the uniform enters limited wear trials this fall, pending final approval from stakeholders who have not yet been told they are stakeholders. Until then, personnel were advised to practice walking in boots without narrating their own entrance music.

When asked if the design might be revised, the president said revisions are for people who do not win, then asked whether the Death Star had ever been properly funded. Aides clarified he was joking. The clarification was not recorded on the official transcript.
