Board Game Cafe Bans Players Who Explain Rules Unsolicited

Zero tolerance for 'actually' before second beer.

By Claire Undertow · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · MADISON · 2 min read

Share Email Post Reprints

MADISON - Hex & Meeple enacted a strict No Rules Lawyers policy Friday after staff documented 200 incidents of unsolicited explanation, with 40% involving Catan and 60% involving someone named Chad. Owner Renee Kwan said the cafe welcomes beginners but not "preemptive pedantry." Offenders receive a yellow card, then temporary ban, then assignment to solo chess where they can explain only to themselves.

The policy was born out of frustration with the constant interruptions during game nights. "I just wanted to roll dice and eat nachos," said player Elena Platt. "Chad wanted a seminar."

When contacted for comment, Chad began clarifying how comments work on social media platforms. He was cut off per policy, but not before he managed to explain that his enthusiasm stemmed from a deep-seated need to feel connected through shared experiences.

Signs now define acceptable help: answer when asked, demonstrate once, do not monologue about house rules from a lake house in 2014. "It's like they're trying to make us play by the book," said regular player Mike Johnson, who was recently issued a yellow card for explaining the rules of Carcassonne too early.

Psychologists say rule-explaining often signals anxiety about control. At Hex & Meeple, they've found an alternative outlet: volunteer shifts where players can explain games to newcomers. "It's like being back in school," said volunteer Sarah Lee, who now finds her passion for game rules channeled into helping others learn.

Revenue has upped since the policy was implemented, mostly from groups returning now that Chad attends a different establishment that "appreciates expertise." Kwan insists the cafe loves games. "We just hate TED Talks with pawns," she said with a straight face.

Special exception granted for staff during official tutorial hour, when beer is half price and eye contact optional. "It's like they're trying to make us play by the book," quipped one regular, who was recently issued a yellow card for explaining the rules of Carcassonne too early.

Repeat offenders receive a copy of the rules and a gentle suggestion to host their own table, preferably in another zip code. "We want everyone to have fun," said Kwan. "But not at our expense."

The policy has sparked some debate among local gamers. Some see it as necessary for maintaining a relaxed atmosphere, while others feel it stifles the natural flow of learning and sharing. Regardless, Hex & Meeple remains committed to its new approach, ensuring that everyone can enjoy their game nights without interruption.

In an unexpected turn of events, the policy has even led to a surge in local game sales as players seek out alternatives. "We've seen a 30% increase in board games sold," said Kwan with a wry smile. "Guess people are looking for a place where they can just play."

As the cafe continues its experiment, one thing is clear: the world of board gaming has never been more... regulated.
