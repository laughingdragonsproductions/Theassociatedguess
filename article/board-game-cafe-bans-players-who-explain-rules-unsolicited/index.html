Board Game Cafe Bans Players Who Explain Rules Unsolicited

Zero tolerance for 'actually' before second beer.

By Claire Undertow · Wednesday, August 19, 2026 · 1 min read

MADISON - Hex & Meeple enacted a strict No Rules Lawyers policy Friday after staff documented 200 incidents of unsolicited explanation, 40% involving Catan and 60% involving someone named Chad.

Owner Renee Kwan said the cafe welcomes beginners but not "preemptive pedantry." Offenders receive a yellow card, then temporary ban, then assignment to solo chess where they can explain only to themselves.

Signs define acceptable help: answer when asked, demonstrate once, do not monologue about house rules from a lake house in 2014.

Regulars applauded. "I just wanted to roll dice and eat nachos," said player Elena Platt. "Chad wanted a seminar."

Chad, reached for comment, began clarifying how comment works. He was cut off per policy.

Psychologists say rule-explaining often signals anxiety about control. Hex & Meeple offers alternative outlet: volunteer shift explaining games to people who opted into learning.

Revenue up since policy, mostly from groups returning now that Chad attends a different establishment that " appreciates expertise."

Kwan insists the cafe loves games. "We just hate TED Talks with pawns."

Special exception granted for staff during official tutorial hour, when beer is half price and eye contact optional.
